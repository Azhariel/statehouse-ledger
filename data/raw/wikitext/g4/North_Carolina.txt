The [[governor of North Carolina]] is the [[head of government]] of the [[U.S. state]] of [[North Carolina]] and commander-in-chief of the state's [[North Carolina National Guard|military forces]].

There have been 70 governors of North Carolina, with six serving non-consecutive terms, totaling 76 terms. The current governor is Democrat [[Josh Stein]], who took office on January 1, 2025.

== Governors ==

=== Presidents of the Council ===
Prior to declaring its independence, North Carolina was a [[Province of North Carolina|colony]] of the [[Kingdom of Great Britain]]. The 13-member Provincial Council, renamed the Council of Safety in April 1776, was essentially the executive authority during the second year of the revolution, and was appointed by the [[North Carolina Provincial Congress|Provincial Congress]]. The Presidency of the Council and the Presidency of the Congress could each be considered the highest offices in the state during this time, but the council was supreme when the congress was not in session.

[[Cornelius Harnett]] served as the first president from October 18, 1775, to August 21, 1776. [[Samuel Ashe (North Carolina governor)|Samuel Ashe]] then served until September 27, 1776, and [[Willie Jones (statesman)|Willie Jones]] until October 25, 1776, at which time a state government had been formed.<ref name="1927manual">{{Cite book |last=North Carolina Secretary of State |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=4dUGAQAAIAAJ |title=North Carolina Manual |date=1927 |publisher=North Carolina Historical Commission |pages=67&ndash;69 |language=en}}</ref>

===State governors===
{{For|the period before independence|List of colonial governors of North Carolina|List of governors of the Province of North Carolina}}
North Carolina was one of the original [[Thirteen Colonies]], and was admitted as a state on November 21, 1789.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Ratification of the Constitution by the State of North Carolina; November 21, 1789 |url=http://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/ratnc.asp |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151123223225/http://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/ratnc.asp |archive-date=November 23, 2015 |access-date=October 31, 2015 |publisher=The Avalon Project at Yale Law School}}</ref> It seceded from the [[Union (American Civil War)|Union]] on May 20, 1861,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Secession Ordinances of 13 Confederate States |url=http://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?smtID=3&psid=3953 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150905091739/http://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?smtID=3&psid=3953 |archive-date=September 5, 2015 |access-date=May 24, 2015 |publisher=[[University of Houston]]}}</ref> and joined the [[Confederate States of America]] on May 21, 1861.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Secession |url=http://northcarolinahistory.org/encyclopedia/secession/ |publisher=John Locke Foundation}}</ref> Following the end of the [[American Civil War]], North Carolina during [[Reconstruction era|Reconstruction]] was part of the [[Second Military District]], which exerted some control over governor appointments and elections. North Carolina was readmitted to the Union on July 4, 1868.<ref>Act authorizing readmission on ratification of 14th amendment: {{usstat|15|73}}. Proclamation of North Carolina's ratification: {{usstat|15|703}}.</ref>

The first constitution of 1776 created the office of governor, to be chosen by the legislature annually, and whom may only serve up to three out of every six years.<ref>1776 Const. art. XV</ref> An amendment in 1835 changed this to popular election for a term of two years,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Constitution, State |url=https://www.ncpedia.org/government/nc-constitution-history |access-date=2023-05-16 |website=www.ncpedia.org}}</ref> allowing them to succeed themselves once.<ref name="1927manual" /> The 1868 constitution lengthened terms to four years, to commence on the first day of January after the election,<ref name="1868 Const. art. III, § 1">1868 Const. art. III, § 1</ref> but they could not succeed themselves,<ref>1868 Const. art. III, § 2</ref> a limit that was changed in 1977 to allow them to succeed themselves once.<ref name="1985manual">{{Cite book |last=North Carolina Secretary of State |url=http://archive.org/details/northcarolinaman1985nort |title=North Carolina manual [serial] |date=1985 |publisher=North Carolina Historical Commission |page=155}}</ref>

The office of [[Lieutenant Governor of North Carolina|lieutenant governor]] was created in 1868, to act as governor when that office is vacant;<ref>1868 Const. art. III, § 12</ref> in 1971 this was changed so that the lieutenant governor becomes governor.<ref>NC Const. art. III, § 3</ref> Before the office was created, the speaker of the senate would act as governor should it be vacant.<ref>1776 Const. art. XIX</ref> The governor and the lieutenant governor are not officially elected on the same ticket.

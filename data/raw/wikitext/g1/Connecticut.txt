The governor of Connecticut is the [[head of government]] of [[Connecticut]], and the commander-in-chief of the [[U.S. state|state's]] [[Connecticut Military Department|military forces]]. The [[Governor (United States)|governor]] has a duty to enforce state laws, and the power to either approve or veto bills passed by the [[Connecticut General Assembly]] and to convene the legislature.{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1965|loc=art. IV}} Unusual among governors, the governor of Connecticut has no power to [[pardon]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Pardons Power in Connecticut |url=http://www.cga.ct.gov/2001/rpt/olr/htm/2001-r-0498.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080613155510/http://www.cga.ct.gov/2001/rpt/olr/htm/2001-r-0498.htm |archive-date=June 13, 2008 |access-date=June 13, 2008 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The governor of Connecticut is automatically a member of the state's Bonding Commission.  He is an ex-officio member of the board of trustees of the [[University of Connecticut]] and [[Yale University]].

There have been 69 post-Revolution governors of the state, serving 73 distinct spans in office. Four have served non-consecutive terms: [[Henry W. Edwards]], [[James E. English]], [[Marshall Jewell]], and [[Raymond E. Baldwin]]. The longest terms in office were in the state's early years, when four governors were elected to nine or more one-year terms. The longest was that of the first governor, [[Jonathan Trumbull]], who served over 14 years, but 7 of those as colonial governor; the longest-serving state governor — with no other position included in the term — was his son, [[Jonathan Trumbull Jr.]], who served over 11 years. The shortest term was that of [[Hiram Bingham III]], who served only one day before resigning to take an elected seat in the [[United States Senate]]. Additionally, [[Lowell Weicker]] is noted for a rare [[third party (U.S. politics)|third party]] win in American politics, having been elected to a term in 1990 representing [[A Connecticut Party]].

The current governor is [[Ned Lamont]], a [[Democratic Party of Connecticut|Democrat]] who took office on January 9, 2019.

== Governors ==
{{For|the period before independence|List of colonial governors of Connecticut}}
Connecticut was one of the original [[Thirteen Colonies]] and was admitted as a state on January 9, 1788.<ref>{{Cite report |title=Admission of States to the Union: A Historical Reference Guide |url=https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R47747 |access-date=October 15, 2026 |id=CRS Report no. R47747 |publisher=[[Congressional Research Service]] |date=December 17, 2024}}</ref> Before it declared its independence, Connecticut was a [[Connecticut Colony|colony]] of the [[Kingdom of Great Britain]].

Connecticut did not create a state constitution for itself until several decades after it became a state; until 1818, the state operated under the provisions of its colonial charter. The charter called for the election of a governor every year, with the term commencing on the second Thursday in May.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=531}}

The current [[Constitution of Connecticut]], ratified in 1965, calls for a four-year term for the governor,{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1965|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ct/constitution/section-id-74123 art. IV, § 1]}} commencing on the Wednesday after the first Monday in the January following an election.{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1965|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ct/constitution/section-id-74125 art. IV, § 2]}} The previous constitution of 1818 originally had only a one-year term for governor;{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=540|loc=1818 art. IV, § 1}} this was increased to two years in 1875,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=551|loc=1875 amend. XVI}} and four years in 1948.{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1818|loc=art. XLV}} The 1875 amendment also set the start date of the term to its current date; before then, it was the first Wednesday in the May following an election.{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1965|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ct/constitution/section-id-74123 art. IV, § 1]}} The constitution provides for the election of a lieutenant governor for the same term as the governor. The two offices are elected on the same [[ticket (election)|ticket]]; this provision was added in 1962.{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1818|loc=amend. VII}} In the event of a vacancy in the office of governor, the lieutenant governor becomes governor.{{sfn|Conn. Const.|1965|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ct/constitution/section-id-74160 art. IV, § 19]}} Before the adoption of the 1965 constitution, the lieutenant governor only acted as governor.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=542|loc=1818 art. IV, § 14}} There is no limit of any kind on the number of terms one may serve.

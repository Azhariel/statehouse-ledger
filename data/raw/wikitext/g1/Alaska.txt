The '''governor of Alaska''' ([[Iñupiaq language|Iñupiaq]]: ''Alaaskam kavanaa'') is the [[head of government]] of [[Alaska]]. The governor is the chief executive of the state and is the holder of the highest office in the executive branch of the government as well as being the commander in chief of [[Alaska Department of Military and Veterans Affairs|Alaska's state forces]].

Twelve people have served as governor of the State of Alaska over 14 distinct terms, though Alaska had over 30 civilian and military governors during its long history as a [[Territories of the United States|United States territory]]. Only two governors, [[William A. Egan]] and [[Bill Walker (American politician)|Bill Walker]], were born in Alaska. Two people, Egan and [[Wally Hickel]], have been elected to multiple non-consecutive terms as governor. Hickel is also noted for a rare [[third party (U.S. politics)|third party]] win in American politics, having been elected to a term in 1990 representing the [[Alaskan Independence Party]]. The longest-serving governor of the state was Egan, who was elected three times and served nearly 12 years. The longest-serving territorial governor was [[Ernest Gruening]], who served over 13 years.

The current governor is Republican [[Mike Dunleavy (politician)|Mike Dunleavy]], who took office on December 3, 2018.

==Governors before statehood==
Alaska was [[Alaska Purchase|purchased]] by the United States from the [[Russian Empire]] in 1867, with formal transfer occurring on October 18, 1867,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Purchase of Alaska, 1867 |url=https://history.state.gov/milestones/1866-1898/alaska-purchase |access-date=October 5, 2026 |website=[[Office of the Historian]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260930225650/https://history.state.gov/milestones/1866-1898/alaska-purchase |archive-date=September 30, 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref> which is now celebrated as [[Alaska Day]]. Before then, it was known as [[Russian colonization of North America|Russian America]] or Russian Alaska, controlled by the [[Russian-American Company#Chief managers|governors and general managers]] of the [[Russian-American Company]].

The vast region was initially designated the [[Department of Alaska]], under the jurisdiction of the [[United States Department of War|Department of War]] until 1877, when the Army was withdrawn from Alaska. The [[United States Department of the Treasury|Department of the Treasury]] then took control, with the Collector of Customs as the highest ranking federal official in the territory. In 1879, the [[United States Navy|Navy]] was given jurisdiction over the department.{{sfn|Gates|2007|p=86}}

Some believe the first American administrator of Alaska was [[Polish people|Polish]] immigrant [[Włodzimierz Krzyżanowski]]. However, the ''[[Anchorage Daily News]]'' was unable to find any conclusive information to support this claim.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Ruskin |first=Liz |location=Washington, D.C. |publication-place=Anchorage, Alaska |date=December 20, 2002 |title=Barking up the wrong Pole: Hero wasn't governor |newspaper=[[Anchorage Daily News]] |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/anchorage-daily-news-barking-up-the-wron/207695135/ |access-date=October 5, 2026 |via=Newspapers.com}}</ref>

===Governors of the District of Alaska===
On May 17, 1884, the Department of Alaska was redesignated the [[District of Alaska]], an incorporated but [[Territories of the United States#Formerly unorganized territories|unorganized territory]] with a civil government.<ref>{{usstat|23|24}}</ref> The governor was appointed by the [[president of the United States]].

==Governors of the State of Alaska==
Alaska was [[Admission to the Union|admitted to the Union]] on January 3, 1959.

The [[Constitution of Alaska|state constitution]] provides for the election of a governor and [[Lieutenant Governor of Alaska|lieutenant governor]] every four years{{sfn|Alaska Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ak/constitution/section-id-44686 art. III, § 4]}} on the same [[Ticket (election)|ticket]],{{sfn|Alaska Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ak/constitution/section-amendment-id-55609 art. III, § 8]}} with their terms commencing on the first Monday in the December following the election.{{sfn|Alaska Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ak/constitution/section-id-44686 art. III, § 4]}} Governors are allowed to succeed themselves once, having to wait four years after their second term in a row before being allowed to run again.{{sfn|Alaska Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ak/constitution/section-id-44688 art. III, § 5]}} Should the office of governor become vacant, the lieutenant governor assumes the office of governor.{{sfn|Alaska Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ak/constitution/section-amendment-id-55617 art. III, § 11]}} The original constitution of 1956 created the office of secretary of state, which was functionally identical to a lieutenant governor, and was renamed to "lieutenant governor" in 1970.{{sfn|Kallenbach|1977|pp=42–43}}

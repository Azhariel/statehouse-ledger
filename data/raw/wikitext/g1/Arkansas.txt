The '''governor of Arkansas''' is the [[head of government]] of the [[U.S. state]] of [[Arkansas]]. The [[Governor (United States)|governor]] is the head of the [[Executive (government)|executive branch]] of the [[Government of Arkansas|Arkansas government]] and is charged with enforcing state laws. They have the power to either approve or veto bills passed by the [[Arkansas General Assembly]], to convene the legislature, and to grant pardons, except in cases of treason and impeachment.{{sfn|Ark. Const.|loc=art. VI}}

The state has had 46 elected governors, as well as 11 acting governors who assumed powers and duties following the resignation or death of the governor. Before becoming a state, [[Arkansas Territory]] had four governors appointed to it by the [[president of the United States]]. [[Orval Faubus]] (1955–1967) served the longest term as governor, being elected six times to serve 12 years. [[Bill Clinton]] (1979–1981; 1983–1992), elected five times over two distinct terms, fell only one month short of 12 years, and [[Mike Huckabee]] (1996–2007) served 10 years for two full four-year terms. The shortest term for an elected governor was the 38 days served by [[John S. Little]] before his [[Mental disorder|nervous breakdown]]; one of the acting successors to his term, [[Jesse M. Martin]], took office only three days before the end of the term, the shortest term overall.

The current governor of Arkansas is [[Republican Party of Arkansas|Republican]] [[Sarah Huckabee Sanders]], who was sworn in on January 10, 2023.

==List of governors==
===Arkansas Territory===
[[Arkansas Territory|Arkansaw Territory]] (renamed Arkansas Territory around 1822) was split from [[Missouri Territory]] on July 4, 1819.<ref>{{usstat|3|493}}</ref> As territorial secretary from 1819 to 1829, [[Robert Crittenden]] served as [[acting governor]] whenever the appointed governor was not in the state. This meant that Crittenden was the first person to perform the duties of governor, since [[James Miller (general)|James Miller]] did not arrive in the territory until nine months after his appointment.<ref name="eoa-crittenden">{{Cite web |last=Bird |first=Allen W. |date=September 16, 2026 |title=Robert Crittenden (1797–1834) |url=https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/robert-crittenden-2270/ |access-date=October 10, 2026 |website=[[Encyclopedia of Arkansas]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260917131915/https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/robert-crittenden-2270/ |archive-date=September 17, 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref>

===State of Arkansas===
Arkansas was [[List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union|admitted to the Union]] on June 15, 1836.<ref>{{usstat|5|50}}</ref> The state [[Secession|seceded]] on May 6, 1861,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Secession Ordinances of 13 Confederate States |url=http://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?smtID=3&psid=3953 |url-status=live |year=2018 |last1=Mintz |first1=S. |last2=McNeil |first2=S. |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150905091739/http://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/disp_textbook.cfm?smtID=3&psid=3953 |archive-date=September 5, 2015 |access-date=October 10, 2026 |website=Digital History}}</ref> and was [[List of Confederate states by date of admission to the Confederacy|admitted to the Confederacy]] on May 21, 1861.<ref>{{cite book |url=https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=dul1.ark:/13960/t2t44fh2r&seq=142 |page=120 |title=The Statutes at Large of the Provisional Government of the Confederate States of America |editor-last=Matthews |editor-first=James M. |year=1864 |location=Richmond, Virginia |publisher=R.M. Smith |via=[[HathiTrust]]}}</ref> When [[Little Rock, Arkansas|Little Rock]], the state capital, was captured on September 10, 1863, the state government relocated to [[Washington, Arkansas]], and a [[Union (American Civil War)|Union]] government was installed in its place, causing an overlap in the terms of Confederate governor [[Harris Flanagin]] and Union governor [[Isaac Murphy]].<ref name="eoa-flanagin" /> During the post-war [[Reconstruction era|Reconstruction period]], it was part of the [[Fourth Military District]]. Arkansas was readmitted to the Union on June 22, 1868.<ref>{{usstat|15|72}}</ref>

The [[Constitution of Arkansas#1836 Constitution|Arkansas Constitution of 1836]] established four-year terms for governors,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=277|loc=1836 art. V, § 4}} which was lowered to two years in the 1874, and current, constitution.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=341|loc=1874 art. VI, § 1}} An amendment in 1984 increased the terms of both governor and [[lieutenant governor]] to four years.{{sfn|Ark. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ar/constitution/section-id-78355 amend. 63]}} Governors were originally limited only to serving no more than eight out of every twelve years,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=277|loc=1836 art. V, § 4}} but the 1874 constitution removed any [[term limit]]. A referendum in 1992 limited governors to two terms.{{sfn|Ark. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ar/constitution/section-id-84245 amend. 73]}}<ref>{{Cite news |last=Wickline |first=Michael R. |date=November 4, 2020 |title=2 issues on state ballot approved by voters |website=[[Arkansas Democrat-Gazette]] |url=https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2020/nov/04/3-issues-on-state-ballot-approved-by-voters/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201104141421/https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2020/nov/04/3-issues-on-state-ballot-approved-by-voters/ |archive-date=November 4, 2020 |url-status=live}}</ref>

Until 1864, the constitutions provided that, should the office of governor be rendered vacant, the [[President of the Senate|president of the senate]] would serve as [[acting governor]] until such time as a new governor was elected or the disability removed, or the acting governor's senate term expired.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=279|loc=1836 art. V, § 18}} This led to some situations where the governorship changed hands in quick succession, due to senate terms ending or new senate presidents being elected. For example, after [[John S. Little]] resigned in 1907, three senate presidents acted as governors before the next elected governor took office.

The 1864 constitution created the office of lieutenant governor{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=298|loc=1864 art. VI, § 19}} who would serve as acting governor in case of vacancy.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=298|loc=1864 art. VI, § 23}} The 1874 constitution removed the position and returned to the original line of succession.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=342|loc=1874 art. VI, § 12}} An amendment to the constitution, passed in 1914 but not recognized until 1925,<ref name="ltgov" /> recreated the office of lieutenant governor, who becomes governor in case of vacancy of the governor's office.<ref>{{cite court |litigants=Bryant v. English |court=Arkansas Supreme Court |vol=311 |reporter=Ark. |opinion=187 |date=1992 |url=https://opinions.arcourts.gov/ark/supremecourt/en/item/247569/index.do |access-date=October 26, 2019}}</ref> The governor and the lieutenant governor are not officially elected on the same [[ticket (election)|ticket]].{{sfn|Blair|Barth|2005|p=169}}

Arkansas was a strongly [[Democratic Party of Arkansas|Democratic]] state before the [[American Civil War|Civil War]], electing only candidates from the Democratic party. It elected three [[Republican Party of Arkansas|Republican]] governors following Reconstruction, but after the Democratic Party re-established control, 92 years passed before voters chose another Republican.

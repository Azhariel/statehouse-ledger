The [[governor of Oregon]] is the [[head of government]] of the [[U.S. state]] of [[Oregon]] and the highest-ranking official in the state.

==List of governors==
The [[Oregon Country]] was obtained by the United States on January 30, 1819,<ref>{{usstat|8|248}}</ref> as a shared region with the [[United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland|United Kingdom]]. The [[Oregon Treaty]] ended the sharing and formally established the borders on June 15, 1846.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Text of "Treaty with Great Britain, in Regard to Limits Westward of the Rocky Mountains" |url=http://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/br-1846.asp |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090419184450/http://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/br-1846.asp |archive-date=April 19, 2009 |access-date=August 4, 2006 |publisher=The Avalon Project at Yale Law School}}</ref>

The [[Champoeg Meetings]], including a constitutional committee, held from February 1841 until May 1843, served as a ''de facto'' government before the government was officially established. While early attempts at establishing a government had been unsuccessful because of discontent between English American and French Canadian settlers over the question of whom they should choose as governor, several other officers were elected at these meetings, including [[Ira Babcock]] as Supreme Judge. For lack of a government, the Supreme Judge also received executive and legislative duties.<ref name="bio">[http://www.oregon.gov/SOLL/docs/1841_1890/Bio/Backcock_Bio.pdf DR. IRA L. BABCOCK], biography from Oregon Government, retrieved 15 May 2017</ref><ref name="History">[https://books.google.com/books?id=_6CdzFrlfn4C&dq=william+johnson+high+sheriff+oregon&pg=PT237 A History of Oregon, 1792-1849], retrieved 15 May 2017</ref>

The meetings at Champoeg led up to the first constitution of the [[Oregon Country]] and several petitions for U.S. territorial status. The resulting acts created a [[Provisional Government of Oregon|provisional government]] on July 5, 1843. The first leaders of this government were an elected three-person [[Executive Committee (Oregon Country)|Executive Committee]]. Later, [[George Abernethy]] was elected governor, and served from June 3, 1845, to March 3, 1849, though this government was never recognized by the federal government.

===Oregon Territory===

The region was organized as [[Oregon Territory]] on August 14, 1848.<ref>{{usstat|9|323}}</ref> During its history it had five governors appointed by the [[president of the United States]].

===State of Oregon===
The state of Oregon was [[admission to the Union|admitted to the Union]] on February 14, 1859.<ref>{{usstat|11|383}}</ref>

The 1857 [[Constitution of Oregon]] provided for the election of a governor every four years, to serve no more than eight out of any twelve years.<ref>OR Const. art. V, § 1</ref> This length and limit have never been changed. It is one of the few states without a lieutenant governor. The office would devolve upon the [[Oregon Secretary of State|secretary of state]] upon vacancy<ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=qz00AQAAMAAJ&pg=PA38 OR Const. art. V, § 8, orig.]</ref> until a 1920 amendment put the president of the Senate first in the line of succession,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Oregon Line of Succession to the Governorship, Measure 5 (May 1920) |url=https://ballotpedia.org/Oregon_Line_of_Sucession_to_the_Governorship,_Measure_5_(May_1920) |access-date=2023-06-09 |website=Ballotpedia |language=en}}</ref> and a 1972 amendment returned the secretary of state to the front.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Oregon Gubernatorial Line of Succession, Measure 8 (1972) |url=https://ballotpedia.org/Oregon_Gubernatorial_Line_of_Succession,_Measure_8_(1972) |access-date=2023-06-09 |website=Ballotpedia |language=en}}</ref>

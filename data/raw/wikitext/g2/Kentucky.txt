The [[governor of Kentucky]] is the [[head of government]] of the Commonwealth of [[Kentucky]].{{sfn|Ky. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ky/constitution/section-id-67583 § 69]}} Fifty-nine individuals have held the office of governor. Prior to a 1992 amendment to the constitution, governors were prohibited from succeeding themselves in office, though four ([[Isaac Shelby]], [[John L. Helm]], [[James B. McCreary]] and [[Happy Chandler]]) served multiple non-consecutive terms. [[Paul E. Patton]], the first Kentucky governor eligible for a second consecutive term under the amendment, won his reelection bid in 1999. [[James Garrard]] succeeded himself in 1800, before the constitutional provision existed. Garrard is also the longest serving governor, serving for a total period of eight years and 90 days. In 2023 [[Kentucky Democratic Party|Democrat]] [[Andy Beshear]] became the 3rd incumbent governor in Kentucky history to be reelected to a second consecutive term.  

[[William Goebel]], who was elected to the office in the disputed [[1899 Kentucky gubernatorial election|election of 1899]], remains the only governor of any U.S. state to die from assassination while in office.<ref>{{Cite encyclopedia |year=1992 |title=Goebel Assassination |encyclopedia=The Kentucky Encyclopedia |publisher=The University Press of Kentucky |location=Lexington |editor-last=Kleber |editor-first=John E. |isbn=0-8131-1772-0}}</ref> Goebel is also the shortest serving governor, serving for a period of only three days. [[Martha Layne Collins]], who held the office from 1983 to 1987, was the first woman to serve as governor and was only the third woman to serve as governor of any U.S. state who had not been married to a previous governor.<ref>{{Cite encyclopedia |year=1992 |title=Governor, Office of |encyclopedia=The Kentucky Encyclopedia |publisher=The University Press of Kentucky |location=Lexington |editor-last=Kleber |editor-first=John E. |isbn=0-8131-1772-0}}</ref> The 63rd and current Kentucky governor is [[Kentucky Democratic Party|Democrat]] [[Andy Beshear]], who took office on December 10, 2019.

==Governors==
[[Kentucky County, Virginia]] was [[admission to the Union|admitted to the Union]] as Kentucky on June 1, 1792. There have been 59 governors, serving 63 distinct terms.

An unelected group proclaimed Kentucky's secession from the [[Union (American Civil War)|Union]] on November 20, 1861, and it was annexed by the [[Confederate States of America]] on December 10, 1861. The Confederate government elected two governors, but it never held much control over the state.

The original 1792 [[Constitution of Kentucky]] had the governor chosen by an [[electoral college]]{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1268|loc=1792 art. II, § 2}} for a term of four years, commencing on the first day of June.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1268|loc=1792 art. II, § 3}} The second constitution in 1799 changed this to a popular vote,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1281|loc=1799 art. III, § 2}} prevented governors from succeeding themselves within seven years of their terms,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1281|loc=1799 art. III, § 3}} and moved the start date to the fourth Tuesday after the election.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1281|loc=1799 art. III, § 5}} The third constitution in 1850 reduced the succession limitation to four years,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1297|loc=1850 art. III, § 3}} and moved the start date of the term to the fifth Tuesday after the election.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1297|loc=1850 art. III, § 5}} A 1992 amendment allowed governors to have a second term before being prevented from succeeding themselves for four years.{{sfn|Ky. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ky/constitution/section-id-67595 § 71]}} Originally, should the office of governor be vacant, the speaker of the Senate would exercise the powers of the office;{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1269|loc=1792 art. II, § 15}} in 1799, the office of lieutenant governor was created to fill this role,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=1282|loc=1799 art. III, § 16–18}} and, as of 1992, is elected on the same [[ticket (election)|ticket]] as the governor.{{sfn|Ky. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/ky/constitution/section-id-67591 § 70]}}

==Confederate governors==

{{stack|
[[File:Gwjohnson ky.jpg|upright|thumb|[[George W. Johnson (Kentucky politician)|George W. Johnson]], 1st Confederate Governor of Kentucky]]
[[File:Richard-Hawes.jpg|upright|thumb|[[Richard Hawes]], 2nd Confederate Governor of Kentucky]]
|clear=right}}

During the [[American Civil War|Civil War]], a group of secessionists met at [[Russellville, Kentucky|Russellville]] to form a [[Confederate government of Kentucky|Confederate government]] for the Commonwealth of Kentucky. This government never successfully displaced the government in [[Frankfort, Kentucky|Frankfort]], and Kentucky remained in the Union through the entire war. Two men were elected governor of the Confederate government: [[George W. Johnson (Kentucky politician)|George W. Johnson]], who served from November 20, 1861, to his death on April 8, 1862, at the [[Battle of Shiloh]], and, on Johnson's death, [[Richard Hawes]], who served until the Confederate surrender on April 9, 1865. The Confederate government disbanded shortly after the end of the war in 1865.<ref>{{Cite encyclopedia |year=1992 |title=Confederate Government |encyclopedia=The Kentucky Encyclopedia |publisher=The University Press of Kentucky |location=Lexington, Kentucky |editor-last=Kleber |editor-first=John E. |isbn=0-8131-1772-0}}</ref>

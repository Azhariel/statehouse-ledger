The [[governor of Illinois]] is the [[head of government]] of the [[U.S. state]] of [[Illinois]]. The [[Governor (United States)|governor]] is the head of the [[Executive (government)|executive branch]] of [[Government of Illinois|Illinois's state government]] and is charged with enforcing state laws. The governor has the power to either approve or [[veto]] bills passed by the [[Illinois General Assembly|Illinois Legislature]], to convene the legislature, and to grant [[pardon]]s, except in cases of [[Impeachment in the United States|impeachment]].{{sfn|Ill. Const.|loc=art. V}} The governor is also the [[commander-in-chief]] of the state's [[Illinois National Guard|military forces]].

Since becoming a state in 1818, 43 people have served as governor of Illinois; before statehood, it had only one territorial governor, [[Ninian Edwards]]. The longest-serving governor was [[Jim Thompson (Illinois politician)|Jim Thompson]], who was elected to four terms lasting 14 years, from 1977 to 1991. Only one governor, [[Richard J. Oglesby]], has served multiple non-consecutive terms, having been elected in 1864, 1872, and 1884.

The current governor is [[JB Pritzker]], who took office on January 14, 2019.

==List of governors==

===Territory of Illinois===
[[Illinois Territory]] was formed on March 1, 1809, from [[Indiana Territory]].<ref>{{usstat|2|514}}</ref> It had only two governors appointed by the [[president of the United States]] before it became a state, and only one ever took office.

===State of Illinois===
Illinois was [[Admission to the Union|admitted to the Union]] on December 3, 1818, consisting of the southern portion of Illinois Territory; the remainder was assigned to [[Michigan Territory]].<ref>{{usstat|3|536}}</ref>

The first [[Constitution of Illinois|Illinois Constitution]], ratified in 1818, provided that a governor be elected every 4 years{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=976|loc=1818 art. III, § 2}} for a term starting on the first Monday in the December following an election.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=976|loc=1818 art. III, § 3}} The constitution of 1848 moved the start of the term to the second Monday in January starting in 1849, thus shortening the term won in the 1844 election to 2 years.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=995|loc=1848 art. IV, § 3}} Governors were not allowed to succeed themselves{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=976|loc=1818 art. III, § 3}} until the 1870 constitution, which removed this limit.

The office of [[Lieutenant Governor of Illinois|lieutenant governor]] was created in the first constitution,{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=977|loc=1818 art. III, § 13}} to exercise the power of governor if that office becomes vacant.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=977|loc=1818 art. III, § 18}} The 1848 constitution changed this to say the power "devolves" upon the lieutenant governor in case of a vacancy.{{sfn|Thorpe|1909|p=997|loc=1848 art. IV, § 19}} The current constitution of 1970 made it so that, in the event of a vacancy, the lieutenant becomes governor,{{sfn|Ill. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/il/constitution/section-id-86871 art. V, § 6]}} and the governor and lieutenant governor are now elected on the same [[ticket (election)|ticket]].{{sfn|Ill. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/il/constitution/section-id-86867 art. V, § 4]}} If the governor feels seriously impeded in performing their job, they can inform the [[secretary of State of Illinois|secretary of state]] and the next in the line of succession, who becomes acting governor until the governor can resume office.{{sfn|Ill. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/il/constitution/section-id-86871 art. V, § 6]}}

The '''governor of Idaho''' is the [[head of government]] of [[Idaho]]{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-id-45116 art. IV, § 5]}} and [[commander-in-chief]] of the [[U.S. state|state]]'s [[Idaho Military Department|military forces]]. The officeholder has the duty to see state laws are executed, power to either approve or veto bills passed by the [[Idaho Legislature]].{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-id-45112 art. IV, § 4]}} The current governor of Idaho is [[Brad Little]], a [[Idaho Republican Party|Republican]], who took office on January 7, 2019.

Thirty-one individuals have held the office of governor of Idaho since the state's admission to the [[United States|Union]] in 1890, two of whom served non-consecutive terms. The state's first governor, [[George L. Shoup]], had the shortest term, of three months; [[Cecil Andrus]] had the longest, of 14 years.

==List of governors==
===Territory of Idaho===
[[Idaho Territory]] was created from [[Dakota Territory]], [[Nebraska Territory]], and [[Washington Territory]] on March 4, 1863.<ref>{{usstat|12|808}}</ref>

There were sixteen territorial governors appointed by the [[president of the United States]] from the territory's organization in 1863 until the formation of the state of [[Idaho]] in 1890. Due to the long distance from [[Washington, D.C.]] to [[Boise, Idaho|Boise]], there was often a lengthy gap between a governor being appointed and his arrival in the territory; three resigned before even arriving.

===State of Idaho===
[[File:2018-07-16 Idaho State Capitol 10.jpg|thumb|right|Office of the Idaho governor]]
[[Idaho]] was [[Admission to the Union|admitted to the Union]] on July 3, 1890. The terms for governor and lieutenant governor are 4 years, commencing on the first Monday in the January following the election.{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-amendment-id-73941 art. IV, § 1]}} Prior to 1946, the offices were elected to terms of two years.{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-id-45104 1890 art. IV, § 1]}} If the office of governor is vacant or the governor is out of state or unable to discharge his duties, the lieutenant governor acts as governor until such time as the disability is removed.{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-id-45139 art. IV, § 12]}} If both the offices of governor and lieutenant governor are vacant or both those officers are unable to fulfill their duties, the President ''pro tempore'' of the [[Idaho Senate]] is next in line, and then the Speaker of the [[Idaho House of Representatives]].{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-id-45143 art. IV, § 14]}} After the change to four-year terms, self-succession (reelection) was not initially allowed; newly elected Governor [[Robert E. Smylie]], formerly the state's attorney general, successfully lobbied the 1955 legislature to propose an amendment to the state constitution to allow gubernatorial reelection, which was approved by voters in 1956.{{sfn|Idaho Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/id/constitution/section-amendment-id-60394 1956 art. IV, § 1]}}<ref>{{Cite news |date=November 7, 1956 |title=Idaho Voters Adopt Three Amendments |page=1 |newspaper=Lewiston Morning Tribune |agency=Associated Press |location=Boise, Idaho |publication-place=Lewiston, Idaho |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=hcpeAAAAIBAJ&sjid=hDIMAAAAIBAJ&pg=3703%2C728300 |access-date=October 22, 2026 |via=[[Google News]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Corlett |first=John |date=March 31, 1963 |title=It's Mystery Why Law Barring Self-Succession Not Repealed |page=5 |location=Boise, Idaho |publication-place=Lewiston, Idaho |newspaper=Lewiston Morning Tribune |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=_cFeAAAAIBAJ&sjid=qTEMAAAAIBAJ&pg=2840,4647264 |access-date=October 22, 2026 |via=[[Google News]]}}</ref> There is no limit to the number of terms a governor may serve.<ref>{{Cite web |date=February 1, 2002 |title=Idaho Makes Term Limits History |url=http://www.ncsl.org/Default.aspx?TabId=14836 |access-date=June 30, 2010 |publisher=National Conference of State Legislatures |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090810223052/http://www.ncsl.org/Default.aspx?TabId=14836 |archive-date=August 10, 2009 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The governor and the lieutenant governor are elected at the same time but not on the same ticket.

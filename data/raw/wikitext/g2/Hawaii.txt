The '''governor of the State of Hawaii''' is the [[head of government]] of [[Hawaii]], and commander-in-chief of the [[U.S. state|state's]] [[Hawaii National Guard|military forces]].{{sfn|Haw. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/hi/constitution/section-id-66145 art. V, § 5]}}

Of the eight governors of the state, two have been elected to three terms, four have been elected to two terms, and one has been elected to one term. No state governor has yet resigned or died in office, nor did any territorial governor die in office. [[George Ariyoshi]] was the first [[Asian Americans|Asian American]] to be governor of any [[U.S. state]]. The current governor is Democrat [[Josh Green (politician)|Josh Green]], who took office on December 5, 2022.

The longest-serving governors are [[John A. Burns]] (1962–1974) and [[George Ariyoshi]] (1974–1986), both of whom served 12 years each.

==List of governors==
The [[Republic of Hawaii]] was annexed by the [[United States]] in 1898. It was organized into Hawaii Territory in 1900, and admitted as a state in 1959. The Republic had only one president, [[Sanford B. Dole]], who later was the first territorial governor.

===Hawaii Territory===
[[Territory of Hawaii|Hawaii Territory]] was [[Territories of the United States|organized]] on June 14, 1900, remaining a territory for 59 years. Twelve people served as territorial governor, appointed by the [[president of the United States]].

===State of Hawaii===

Hawaii was admitted to the Union on August 21, 1959, consisting of Hawaii Territory minus [[Palmyra Atoll]]. Since then, there have been nine governors.

The governor is elected to a four-year term commencing on the first Monday in the December following the election. The [[Lieutenant Governor of Hawaii|lieutenant governor]] is elected for the same term and, since 1964, on the same [[ticket (election)|ticket]] as the governor.{{sfn|Haw. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/hi/constitution/section-id-66134 art. V, § 1]}}<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Tuttle Jr. |first=Daniel W. |date=June 1967 |title=The 1966 Election in Hawaii |journal=The Western Political Quarterly |volume=20 |issue=2, part 2 |pages=563–567 |doi=10.2307/446083 |jstor=446083}}</ref>  The [[1978 Hawaii State Constitutional Convention|1978 constitutional convention]] established a [[term limit]] of two consecutive terms for both offices.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/honolulu-star-bulletin-big-success-of-co/208719043/ |title=Big Success of Con Con Is Surprise |date=November 8, 1978 |newspaper=[[Honolulu Star-Bulletin]] |last=Gomes |first=Lee |page=1 |via=Newspapers.com}}</ref> If the office of governor is vacant, the lieutenant governor becomes governor; if the governor is out of the state or unable to fulfill duties, the lieutenant governor acts as governor during such absence or disability.{{sfn|Haw. Const.|loc=[https://50constitutions.org/hi/constitution/section-id-66143 art. V, § 4]}}

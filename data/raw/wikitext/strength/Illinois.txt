{{Short description|Politics in the US state of Illinois}}
'''Illinois''' is a [[Red states and blue states|Democratic stronghold]] in presidential elections and one of the "Big Three" Democratic strongholds alongside [[Political party strength in California|California]] and [[Political party strength in New York (state)|New York]]. It is one of the most Democratic states in the nation with all state executive offices and both state legislative branches held by Democrats.<ref>{{cite news |last1=O'connor |first1=John |title=Gov. Pritzker gives Democrats near-record power in Illinois |url=https://www.apnews.com/b323ca25bc2c4c369a83b32cc9fc3f63 |accessdate=15 January 2019 |work=AP NEWS |date=14 January 2019}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Illinois: The Most Democratic State|url=https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/local/illinois-the-most-democratic-state/1907744/|access-date=2021-06-21|website=NBC Chicago|language=en-US}}</ref> For most of its history, Illinois was widely considered to be a swing state, voting for the winner of all but two presidential elections in the 20th century. '''Political party strength in Illinois''' is highly dependent upon [[Cook County, Illinois|Cook County]], and the state's reputation as a blue state rests upon the fact that over 40% of its population and political power is concentrated in [[Chicago]], Cook County, and the [[Chicago metropolitan area]]. Outside of Chicago, the suburban [[collar counties]] continue trending Democratic<ref>{{cite news |last1=Baker |first1=Suzanne |title=How a blue wave rolled through historically Republican Naperville, leaving the GOP in its wake |url=https://www.chicagotribune.com/suburbs/naperville-sun/ct-nvs-midterm-results-by-precinct-st-1111-story.html |access-date=3 September 2021 |work=chicagotribune.com}}</ref> while downstate Illinois can be considered more conservative with several Democratic leaning regions including [[Champaign-Urbana]], [[Bloomington-Normal]], [[Rockford, Illinois|Rockford]], [[Peoria, Illinois|Peoria]], the [[Quad Cities]], and suburban [[St. Louis]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Smith |first1=Katlyn |title=Democrats gain ground in once solidly red DuPage County |url=https://www.dailyherald.com/news/20201104/democrats-gain-ground-in-once-solidly-red-dupage-county |accessdate=9 November 2020 |work=Daily Herald |date=4 November 2020}}</ref>

Illinois's electoral college votes have gone towards the Democratic presidential candidate for the past eight elections, and its congressional makeup tilts heavily Democratic with a [[List of United States representatives from Illinois|14–3 majority]] {{as of|2026|lc=yes}}. However, it has a history of competitive statewide elections and has elected a small number of moderate Republicans including Governors [[Jim Edgar]], [[George Ryan]], and [[Bruce Rauner]], Senators [[Peter Fitzgerald (politician)|Peter Fitzgerald]] and [[Mark Kirk]], and other state executive officeholders including [[Judy Baar Topinka]] and [[Dan Rutherford]].

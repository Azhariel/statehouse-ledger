{{Short description|Politics in the US state of Delaware}}
{{more citations needed|date=January 2021}}
[[Delaware]] is a state in the Mid-Atlantic region of the United States.<ref>{{cite web |title=Census Regions and Divisions of the United States |url=https://www2.census.gov/geo/pdfs/maps-data/maps/reference/us_regdiv.pdf |access-date=March 19, 2025 |website=[[United States Census Bureau]]}}</ref> The [[Government of Delaware]] is composed of the executive branch, legislative branch, and judicial branch.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Departments of Government Chapter 90C. Department of Technology and Information |url=https://delcode.delaware.gov/title29/c090c/sc01/index.html |access-date=2025-03-19 |website=The Delaware Code Online}}</ref> Delaware's executive branch has five elected offices, they are [[Governor of Delaware|Governor]], [[Lieutenant Governor of Delaware|Lieutenant Governor]], [[Attorney General of Delaware|Attorney General]], State Treasurer, [[Delaware Auditor of Accounts|Auditor of Accounts]], and [[Delaware Department of Insurance|Insurance Commissioner]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Voting in Delaware |url=https://delaware.gov/guides/voting-in-delaware/ |access-date=2025-03-19 |website=[[Delaware.gov]] |language=en-US}}</ref> The [[Delaware General Assembly|state legislature]] is made of the [[Delaware House of Representatives]] and [[Delaware Senate]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Session Laws |url=https://legis.delaware.gov/SessionLaws |access-date=2025-03-19 |website=[[Delaware General Assembly]]}}</ref> From 1776 to 1792, the position of Governor was known as President.<ref name=":1" /> 

Additionally, the state sends two [[United States Senate|senators]] and one [[Representative (US)|representative]] to the [[United States Congress]].<ref name=":0" /> All three seats are elected [[at-large]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=2024-11-12 |title=Delaware At-Large Congressional District Election Results |url=https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2024/11/05/us/elections/results-delaware-us-house-at-large.html |access-date=2025-03-19 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=How Our Laws Are Made - Congress.gov Resources |url=https://www.congress.gov/help/learn-about-the-legislative-process/how-our-laws-are-made |access-date=2025-03-19 |website=[[Congress.gov]]}}</ref> As of the [[2024 United States House of Representatives election in Delaware|2024 House of Representatives election]], the state's representative is [[Sarah McBride]], the first openly transgender person elected to Congress.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Karni |first=Annie |date=2025-03-11 |title=Republican Refers to Transgender Member as a Man, Cutting Short a House Hearing |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/03/11/us/politics/sarah-mcbride-keith-self-transgender.html |access-date=2025-03-19 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref>

In the 1960s and 1970s the state's politics were dominated by the Republican party.<ref name=":2" /> In the 1980s the state began to shift towards the Democratic party.<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Corty |first1=Susanne P. |last2=Hanson |first2=Joseph J. |date=November 4, 1982 |title=The Governor returns to bitter defeat |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-morning-news-the-governor-returns-to/186589581/ |access-date=2025-03-19 |website=[[The News Journal]] |page=18 |language=en |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> Writing for ''[[The News Journal]]'', William B. Golin credited the shift towards the Democratic party to the upset victory of then-County Councilman [[Joe Biden]] in the [[1972 United States Senate election in Delaware|1972 United States Senate election]].<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Golin |first=William B. |title=How Joe Biden changed Delaware's — and America's — politics |url=https://www.delawareonline.com/story/opinion/2020/11/27/how-joe-biden-changed-delawares-and-americas-politics/6421726002/ |access-date=2025-03-19 |website=The News Journal |language=en-US}}</ref>

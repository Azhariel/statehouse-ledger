California was a former [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]] leaning state until the 1990s, but since then has become a [[Red states and blue states|stronghold]] of the  [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic]] and considered to be one of the "Big Three" Democratic strongholds alongside [[Political party strength in New York (state)|New York]] and [[Political party strength in Illinois|Illinois]]. Originally a [[swing state]] following statehood, California began regularly supporting Republicans for the first half of the 20th century. Republican dominance waned with demographic change, accelerating with increased immigration beginning in the 1980s. The party remained competitive with Democratic candidates until [[1992 United States presidential election|1992]]. This changed amidst a struggling economy, when California flipped from Republican to elect Democrat [[Bill Clinton]] as President. Republicans still won statewide contests in the state until the late 2000s, with [[Arnold Schwarzenegger]] elected governor.
